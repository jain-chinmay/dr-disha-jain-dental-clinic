What is dentures?

Dentures are removable teeth. Complete dentures replace all the teeth in an arch; partial dentures fill gaps and clip onto the remaining teeth.

A good denture depends on careful measurements and follow-up adjustments, since gums change shape over time.

Who is it for?

  • Multiple or all teeth missing
  • Teeth that cannot be saved
  • People who want a non-surgical option
  • Replacing old, loose or ill-fitting dentures

What happens, step by step

  1. Examination and discussion of options
  2. Any needed extractions or gum healing
  3. Impressions and measurements
  4. Try-in to check fit, bite and appearance
  5. Fitting the final dentures
  6. Follow-up adjustments for comfort

Benefits

Restores chewing

Eat a wider variety of food again.

Clearer speech

Replacing missing teeth helps with pronunciation.

Supports your face

Teeth give shape and support to lips and cheeks.

Affordable option

A practical option for many missing teeth.

Aftercare
  • Remove and clean dentures daily
  • Soak them overnight in a denture solution or water
  • Do not use hot water or harsh cleaners
  • Expect a few days of adjustment; return for fine-tuning
  • Review the fit every year

Frequently asked questions

How long to get used to dentures?

Most people adapt within a few weeks. Soreness spots can be eased with simple adjustments.

Can I eat normally?

Start with soft food and cut it small. Most people gradually return to a normal diet.

Do dentures need replacing?

Gums and bone change over time, so dentures may need relining or replacing after some years.

Are implants an alternative?

Yes, implants or implant-supported dentures can give extra stability. We can discuss whether they suit you.
