Root canal treatment in Palam Vihar, Gurugram

Root canal treatment (RCT) removes infected or inflamed tissue from inside a tooth so you can keep it. At Dr. Disha Jain's clinic in Palam Vihar, Gurugram, the tooth is numbed first and every step is explained.

What is root canal treatment?

Inside every tooth is a soft core called the pulp, which holds nerves and blood vessels. Deep decay, a crack or an injury can let bacteria reach it, causing pain, swelling and infection.

In a root canal, the dentist removes the damaged pulp, cleans and shapes the canals, and seals them. The tooth stays in your mouth and keeps working, usually with a crown on top for protection.

Who is it for?

  • Severe or throbbing toothache, especially at night
  • Lingering pain or sensitivity to hot and cold
  • Swelling or a pimple-like bump on the gum
  • A tooth that has darkened after an injury
  • Deep decay close to the nerve

What happens, step by step

  1. Examination and X-ray to see the shape of the roots and the extent of infection
  2. Local anaesthesia so the area is fully numb
  3. Opening the tooth and removing the infected pulp
  4. Cleaning, disinfecting and shaping the canals
  5. Filling and sealing the canals
  6. A filling or crown to restore the tooth's strength

Benefits

Saves your own tooth

Keeping the natural tooth is usually better for chewing and for the neighbouring teeth.

Ends the infection pain

Removing the source of infection is what relieves the ache.

Comfortable procedure

With proper numbing, most people describe it as similar to a long filling.

Long-lasting with care

A well-sealed and well-restored tooth can last many years.

Aftercare
  • Avoid chewing hard food on that side until the final filling or crown is placed
  • Take any prescribed medicines as directed
  • Mild soreness for a day or two is common
  • Brush and floss as usual, and keep your follow-up visit
  • Call us if swelling or severe pain returns

Frequently asked questions

Is a root canal painful?

The tooth is numbed before we begin, so the procedure itself is usually comfortable. The pain people fear is mostly from the infection that the treatment relieves.

How many visits does it take?

Often one to three visits, depending on the tooth and the infection. We will tell you the plan after the examination.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

Back teeth usually benefit from a crown because they take heavy chewing force. Dr. Jain will advise based on how much of your tooth remains.

Can I eat normally afterwards?

Wait until the numbness wears off. Avoid hard food on that side until the tooth is fully restored.

What if I ignore the pain?

Infection can spread and the tooth may eventually need extraction. Earlier treatment is simpler and saves more of the tooth.
