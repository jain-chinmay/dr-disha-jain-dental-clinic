What is wisdom tooth removal?

Wisdom teeth are the last molars, usually appearing in the late teens or twenties. Many people do not have enough space for them.

Impacted wisdom teeth can trap food, cause infection and decay in the tooth next to them. Not every wisdom tooth needs removal, and Dr. Jain will tell you honestly if yours can stay.

Who is it for?

  • Repeated pain or swelling around the back teeth
  • A wisdom tooth growing sideways or only partly erupted
  • Decay in a wisdom tooth or the one in front
  • Gum infection around a partly erupted tooth

What happens, step by step

  1. Examination and an X-ray (often an OPG) to see the tooth and roots
  2. Local anaesthesia
  3. Removing the tooth, sometimes in sections if it is impacted
  4. Cleaning and closing the area if needed
  5. Written aftercare instructions

Benefits

Removed only when needed.

Aftercare

  • Use cold compresses for the first day
  • Eat soft food and avoid hot drinks for 24 hours
  • Do not rinse forcefully
  • Take medicines as prescribed
  • Report fever, heavy bleeding or worsening pain

  • Number of wisdom teeth

Frequently asked questions

Do all wisdom teeth need removing?

No. If they are healthy, fully erupted and easy to clean, they can often stay.

Is it very painful?

The area is numbed, so you should not feel pain during the procedure. Some swelling and soreness afterwards is expected.

How long is recovery?

Most people feel better within a few days, with full healing in a couple of weeks.
