What is tooth extraction?

Dr. Jain will always check whether a tooth can be saved first. When it cannot, extraction relieves pain and prevents infection spreading.

Simple extractions remove a tooth that is visible and loose enough to lift out. Surgical extractions are for broken or deeply placed teeth.

Who is it for?

  • A badly broken or decayed tooth
  • An infected tooth that cannot be restored
  • Loose teeth from advanced gum disease
  • Crowding, when planned for orthodontic or denture reasons

What happens, step by step

  1. Examination and X-ray
  2. Local anaesthesia
  3. Loosening and removing the tooth
  4. Cleaning the area and controlling bleeding
  5. Aftercare instructions and a replacement plan

Aftercare

  • Bite on the gauze for the time advised
  • Do not rinse or spit forcefully for 24 hours
  • Eat soft, cool food for the first day
  • Avoid smoking, which delays healing
  • Call us if bleeding or pain increases after a few days

  • Position of the tooth
  • Whether X-rays are needed

Frequently asked questions

Will it hurt?

The area is numbed, so you should feel pressure but not pain. Some soreness afterwards is normal.

How long to heal?

The gum usually heals in one to two weeks; the bone takes longer to fill in.

Do I have to replace the tooth?

Not always, but gaps can cause neighbouring teeth to drift. We will discuss options.
