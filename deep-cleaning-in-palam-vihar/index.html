What is deep cleaning (root planing)?

When gum disease sets in, pockets form between teeth and gums where bacteria collect. Regular brushing cannot reach them.

Scaling and root planing cleans these pockets thoroughly, usually in one or more visits under local anaesthesia.

Who is it for?

  • Bleeding, swollen or receding gums
  • Persistent bad breath
  • Gum pockets found at a check-up
  • Loose-feeling teeth

What happens, step by step

  1. Gum examination and measurement of pockets
  2. Local anaesthesia
  3. Cleaning below the gum line
  4. Smoothing the root surfaces
  5. Review of healing and home-care advice

Benefits

Treats the cause

Removes the bacteria driving gum disease.

Helps gums heal

Less bleeding and swelling.

Protects teeth

Slows loss of supporting bone.

Comfortable

Done under numbing.

Aftercare

  • Mild soreness for a few days
  • Use a soft brush
  • Rinse as advised
  • Keep follow-up reviews
  • Maintain very good home cleaning

Frequently asked questions

Is deep cleaning painful?

The area is numbed, so it should be comfortable. Gums can be tender afterwards.

How many visits?

One to several, depending on how much of the mouth needs treatment.

Will my gums grow back?

Gums that have receded do not grow back, but treatment stops the disease from getting worse.
